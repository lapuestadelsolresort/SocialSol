## 1. Who they are

People-ops leads, executive assistants, founders, or office-of-the-COO operators at companies of roughly 10–60 people who run an annual or biannual off-site. Often the budget owner is also the planner — there's no separate "events team." They book once or twice a year and remember the venue for years if it lands well.

The retreat being planned is usually 3–5 days, weekday-heavy (the company doesn't want to burn weekends), with a mix of structured working sessions and unstructured social time. Group size 10–40. Budget supports a private buyout when the math works against per-person hotel + meeting room + dinner-out arithmetic.

## 3. What they care about

Ranked. Top item is the one this email most plausibly speaks to.

1. **Will this venue get out of our way?** The retreat planner needs the property to handle logistics so they can focus on the agenda. Surprises around food, rooms, or noise from other guests sink the trip.
2. **Can the team actually be together?** A retreat where half the group sleeps in one hotel and half across the street is not a retreat. Single-property housing is the load-bearing requirement.
3. **Is there workable space for the structured part?** Indoor and outdoor space for 10–40 people with chairs, a wall to project on or write on, and a good wifi signal. Not a conference center; a usable room.
4. **Does the unstructured time deliver?** Pool, beach, food, evening atmosphere. The retreat is half social bonding; if the property is dead at 7 pm, the retreat fails.

**Open for Sarah:** confirm the ranking; correct anything that doesn't match how you'd talk to a retreat organizer.

## 4. Pain points we credibly solve

- **Pain: split lodging breaks the retreat.** Most properties at this group size require an overflow hotel.
  - **How we solve it:** Full-resort buyout, six villas, sleeps up to 26 in beds + 7 rollaways. The team stays together.
- **Pain: weekday availability is harder to find than weekend availability.** Retreats are weekday by design; many wedding-heavy properties only release weekdays at a discount or not at all.
  - **How we solve it:** Weekday availability is the norm here, not the exception. Buyouts Monday–Friday are routinely on offer.
- **Pain: catering coordination eats the planner's time.** Retreats with three meals a day across 4 days = 12 meal events to coordinate with outside vendors.
  - **How we solve it:** Chef Daniel and team handle three meals/day on-property. BYO alcohol welcome; bar service available at $35/hr per bartender.
- **Pain: privacy + focus.** Other guests, retail traffic, or staff overflow disrupt the retreat container.
  - **How we solve it:** Genuinely private oceanfront resort during a buyout. The team is the only group on property.

## 9. Hook angles

- **Shared region:** company is HQ'd in CA / TX / NY metro / MX-adjacent — annual off-site is a reasonable thing to mention.
- **Recent event reference:** the company posted publicly about a retreat or off-site (LinkedIn, blog, press release). Composer must NOT fabricate; only used when enrichment surfaces a real reference.
- **Soft question:** opens with a planning-relevant question ("Is your team's next off-site already locked, or still in a window where a venue intro is useful?").
- **Direct intro:** no hook; clear who-we-are + why-them + link. Used when enrichment is thin.
- **Mutual context:** if enrichment surfaces a shared portfolio company, an investor in common, or a mutual operator network.

## 10. Structure (the composer's scaffolding)

4. **CTA:** 1 sentence. Link to landing page (once provisioned) with one-line framing — see `library/shared/cta-variants.md` for tonal shape.
5. **Sign-off:** rotate per §8.

Total: 40–150 words, 3–6 sentences. Within `constraints.json` `length`.
